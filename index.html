⚠ Election News Alert
  • Post Office Mail-Ballot Handling: Slower Mail, a Late-Postmark Risk, and USPS's "Extraordinary Measures" Come Late: Votebeat (Oct. 12) and NPR (Oct. 9) report that USPS's Regional Transportation Optimization plan, rolled out since April 2025, cut daily pickups at post offices more than 50 miles from a processing center, so a ballot dropped in a rural mailbox may not be postmarked until the next day. USPS says it is confident it will deliver election mail and urges voters to mail ballots at least a week before their state's deadline. Its extra steps start later than in 2022 and 2024: some post offices extend hours and set up manual-postmark lines from Oct. 24, and RTO-affected offices separate ballots from other mail so they can be postmarked the day they are collected starting Oct. 26. Evidence from the 2026 primaries is worrying: Oregon rejected more than 7,300 ballots for improper postmarks in May (a 13% rate, up from 9.5% in 2024), late-postmark rejections rose more than 300% in some Washington counties, and Democracy Docket found roughly 130,000 of 205,000 rejected mail ballots across six states were late. A Brookings analysis estimates RTO could cause 43,000 to 55,000 additional ballots to be rejected as late this November. No nationwide on-time delivery data has been published. Election officials recommend drop boxes or in-person return. The Supreme Court's Sept. 14 order blocked the Trump effort to let USPS reject ballots. Read the Votebeat report · NPR on postmarks
